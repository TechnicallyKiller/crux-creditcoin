# CRUX — logo prompt

Paste everything below the line into an image or design tool.

---

Design a logomark and wordmark for **CRUX**.

## What CRUX is

A settlement engine on Creditcoin. A contract states its resolution rule as
**data** — which chain, which contract, which event, which field, which
comparison, which block window — and CRUX settles it against a **cryptographic
proof that the event actually occurred on Ethereum**, verified by a native
precompile inside a single block. No oracle, no committee, no admin key, no
dispute window.

Its first application is prediction markets that settle themselves. The mark
should express **proof and finality**, not betting. No dice, no charts, no coins,
no scales, no crystal balls.

The name means *the decisive point* — the thing everything else hangs on.

## The visual world it belongs to

The whole product is built in the language of **security engraving**: banknotes,
share certificates, passports, bearer bonds. Guilloche rosettes, intaglio
line-work, microtext, wax seals, serial numbers. That language was invented for
exactly one purpose — patterns a forger cannot reproduce, so a document can be
verified by looking at it. A cryptographic proof is the same idea done with
mathematics, so the metaphor is structural rather than decorative.

The mark must look native to that world: **engraved, precise, drawn with a
compass and a ruling pen**. Not illustrated, not 3D, not gradient-filled.

## Direction — a guilloche rosette that resolves into a cross

The strongest idea, and the one I want explored first:

**A guilloche rosette whose interference pattern forms an X at its centre.**

Concentric epitrochoid curves — radius modulated as the angle sweeps, the way a
rose engine cuts a banknote lathe pattern — arranged so the negative space where
the lobes meet reads unmistakably as a **crux**: a crossing, an X, the decisive
point where two things intersect.

Read it two ways, both true:
- the **rosette** is authenticity you can see, the thing a forger cannot copy;
- the **cross** is the meeting point of two chains — Ethereum's truth and
  Creditcoin's settlement — and the X that marks a decision.

At large sizes the engraving is visible and intricate. At 16px it must collapse
to a clean, confident X-in-a-circle that is still legible in a browser tab.
**Design it from the favicon outward, not the other way round.**

## Alternative directions, if the first does not land

**A seal caught mid-strike.** A circular seal whose rim is complete but whose
interior engraving is drawn only partway — proof in progress, the plate about to
be struck. Captures that verification is a *process with a moment*.

**A single unbroken line that closes.** One continuous engraved stroke that
travels and returns to its own origin, closing the loop exactly. A proof is a
chain of steps that must close; if it does not close, there is no proof.
Minimal, and it animates beautifully — the loop drawing itself is the app's
existing verification gesture.

## Wordmark

**CRUX** in a high-contrast display serif — a Didone or a sharp transitional,
the kind of face cut into share certificates. Wide letterspacing, generous
tracking, small enough weight that the hairlines stay hairlines. It should read
as *engraved into* the surface, not printed on top of it.

The pairing already in use is Bodoni Moda for propositions, Archivo for
interface, JetBrains Mono for anything checkable. The wordmark should sit with
Bodoni Moda without being a straight setting of it.

## Colour

```
ink-indigo   #0B0F26   the ground — passport interior, not crypto-black
vellum       #EDEFE3   the paper — cool, NOT warm cream
lime         #C8FF3D   ultraviolet security ink
```

**The lime is UV ink and that is load-bearing.** On a banknote, UV ink is
invisible until you check under the right light — it only reveals itself when
somebody verifies. In this product lime appears *only* where a cryptographic
proof has verified, never as a general accent.

So: the primary mark is **single-colour**, vellum on indigo or indigo on vellum.
Lime is reserved for a *verified* state of the mark — the proven variant — and
must never be the default logo colour. If the mark needs lime to look good, it
is the wrong mark.

## Deliverables

- the logomark alone, single colour, as pure vector line-work
- the mark locked up with the CRUX wordmark, horizontal and stacked
- a favicon-scale test at 16px, 32px and 48px, actually rendered at those sizes
- the *proven* variant, where lime enters
- everything on both indigo and vellum grounds

## Avoid

Gradients, glow, bevels, 3D, glassmorphism. Blockchains drawn as cubes or chain
links. Hexagons. Shield icons. Padlocks. Checkmarks in circles. Anything that
would look at home on a generic crypto exchange.

The test: it should look like something a **mint** would put on a plate — a
hundred years old and completely modern at the same time.
